EARLY HOURS

WAKE UPIn gassho, recite the following: “Waking from sleep, the Bodhisattva inspires all beings to awaken to All-Knowledge, gazing upon the ten directions.”

FACE WASHINGGassho-bow to the bathroom altar.

When picking up the toothbrush, “Taking the toothbrush in hand, the Bodhisattva inspires all beings to attain the wonderful teaching and be ultimately pure.”

When brushing the teeth, “Brushing the teeth, the Bodhisattva inspires all beings to be harmonious and pure in mind, biting through all afflictions.”

When rinsing the mouth, “Using water to rinse the mouth, the Bodhisattva inspires all beings to approach the Gate of the Pure Dharma and accomplish liberation.”

When washing the face, “Using water to wash the face, the Bodhisattva inspires all beings to obtain the Gate of the Pure Dharma, forever without stain.”

Gassho-bow to the bathroom altar before leaving.

DAWN ZAZENBow to the altar and approach from the left by stepping sideways. Offer fresh water and light the candle. Light a stick of incense, place the foot of the incense to the forehead and offer it to the Buddha, then place it in the incensor. Bow and leave on the right by stepping sideways. Bow to the cushion, turn clockwise, and bow to the room. Sit and turn clockwise, facing the wall. Ring the meditation bell three times to begin and once to end.

DONNING KESAPlace the folded kesa or rakusu on the head and hold hands in gassho while reciting the Verse of the Kesa three times: “How great, the robe of liberation, a formless field of merit. Wrapping ourselves in Buddha’s teaching, we free all living beings.”

MORNING SUTRA CHANTINGTurn to sit facing the altar. Offer incense. Recite seven times, “Gya tei gya tei. Ha ra gya tei. Hara so gya tei. Bo ji sowa ka.”

Eko Text for Morning Sutra Chanting: “Chanting the Heart Sutra Mantra, we dedicate this merit to the pure body of the dharma realm, and to our home's protective spirits. We pray for our family's peace, for their health and longevity, and for the awakening of all beings. Ji ho san shi i shi fu, shi son bu sa mo ko sa, mo ko ho ja ho ro mi.”

Three Prostrations.

TOILETOffer incense to the bathroom altar and bow. “Going to the toilet, the Bodhisattva inspires all beings to reject greed, hatred, and ignorance, and cleanse all transgressions.”

When washing the hands, “Using water to wash the hands, the Bodhisattva inspires all beings to obtain pure hands to receive and uphold the Buddha-Dharma.”

MORNING

MORNING MEALBefore eating, perform the five contemplations with hands in gassho: “We reflect on the effort that brought us this food and consider how it comes to us. We reflect on our virtue and practice, and whether we are worthy of this offering. We regard greed as the obstacle to freedom of mind. We regard this meal as medicine to sustain our life. For the sake of enlightenment, we now receive this food.”

After eating, recite in gassho: “Abiding in this ephemeral world like a lotus in muddy water, the mind is pure and goes beyond. Thus we bow to Buddha.” Bow.

AFTER-BREAKFAST COMMUNAL LABOREngage in mindful work to maintain the household.

SHOWER AND DRESS“Bathing the body, the Bodhisattva inspires all beings to have hearts and bodies without defilement, pure and bright inside and out.” Bow three times to the bathroom altar.

COMMUTE“Setting out on a road, the Bodhisattva inspires all beings to proceed toward the Buddha’s conduct and enter the place of no-reliance.”

WORK PRACTICE"Seeing a person of Right Livelihood, the Bodhisattva inspires all beings to attain pure livelihood, and not affect a virtuous manner."

MIDDAY

KANKIN SUTRA READINGOffer incense and recite Sutra-Opening Verse: “The unsurpassed, profound, and wondrous Dharma is rarely met with, even in a hundred, thousand, million kalpas. Now we can see and hear it, accept and maintain it. May we unfold the meaning of the Tathagata's truth.”

Read Chapter 5 of The Meaning of Practice and Verification (Shushōgi 修證義): “V. Practicing Buddhism and Repaying Blessings

“26. ◎ Arousing the thought of enlightenment is mainly something that human beings in this world should do. Should we not rejoice that we have had the opportunity to be born in this land of the Buddha Shakyamuni and to have encountered him?

“27. We should calmly consider that if this was a time when the true dharma had not yet spread in the world, we would not be able to encounter it, even if we vowed to sacrifice our very lives for it. We who have at present encountered the true dharma should make such a vow. Do we not know that the Buddha said, "When you meet their teachers who expound supreme enlightenment, do not consider their family background, do not regard their appearance, do not dislike their faults, and do not think about their conduct. Simply, out of respect for wisdom, bow to them three times daily, honor them, and do not cause them any grief."

“28. That we are now able to see the Buddha and hear the dharma is due to the blessings that have come to us through the practice of every one of the buddhas and ancestors. If the buddhas and ancestors had not directly transmitted the dharma, how could it have reached us today? We should be grateful for the blessings of even a single phrase; we should be grateful for the blessings of even a single dharma. How much more should we be grateful for the great blessings of the treasury of the eye of the true dharma, the supreme great dharma. The injured bird did not forget its blessings, but showed its thanks with the rings of three ministries. The trapped tortoise did not forget its blessings, but showed its thanks with the seal ofYubu. If even animals repay their bless­ings, how could humans ignore them?

“29. Our expression of gratitude should not consist in any other practices; the true path of such expression lies solely in our daily practice of Buddhism. This means that we practice without neglecting our lives day to day and without being absorbed in ourselves.

“30. Time flies faster than an arrow, and life is more transient than the dew. With what skillful means or devices can we retrieve even a single day that has passed? A hundred years lived to no purpose are days and months to be regretted. It is to be but a pitiful bag of bones. Even if we live in abandon, as slaves to the senses for the days and months of a hundred years, if we take up practice for a single day therein, it is not only the practice of this life of a hundred years, but also salvation in the hundred years of another life. The life of this day is a life that should be esteemed, a bag of bones that should be honored. We should love and respect our bodies and minds, which undertake this practice. Depending on our practice, the practice of the buddhas is manifested, and the ◎ great way of the buddhas penetrates everywhere. Therefore, the practice of a single day is the seed of the buddhas, the practice of the buddhas.

“31. These buddhas are the Buddha Shakyamuni. The Buddha Shakyamuni is "mind itself is buddha." When buddhas of the past, present and future together fulfill buddhahood, they always become the Buddha Shakya­muni. This is "mind itself is buddha." We should care­fully investigate who is meant when we say ● "mind itself is buddha." ● This is how we repay the blessings of the Buddha.”

Recite Universal Transference of Merit: “May this merit extend universally to all, so that we together with all beings realize the Buddha way. Ji ho san shi i shi fu, shi son bu sa mo ko sa, mo ko ho ja ho ro mi.”

We pray for a peaceful home, a stable lay practice, and auspicious conditions for all. Ji ho san shi i shi fu, shi son bu sa mo ko sa, mo ko ho ja ho ro mi.”

EVENING

EVENING SUTRA CHANTINGOffer incense and chant seven times: “Shi do nī, o kya rā, mi ri shū, ho ri to ra yā, ni ke rī.”

Eko Text for Evening Sutra Chanting: “Chanting the Surangama Heart Mantra, we dedicate this merit to the pure body of the dharma realm, to the myriad spirits, and to our kitchen guardian. We pray for a safe and peaceful household and for the flourishing of the Dharma through our actions. Ji ho san shi i shi fu, shi son bu sa mo ko sa, mo ko ho ja ho ro mi.”

EVENING MEALSpend this time mindfully sharing food with family. Be present in their lives. Follow with a thorough cleaning of the kitchen together.

FOUR VOWSBeings are numberless; I vow to free them. Delusions are inexhaustible; I vow to end them. Dharma gates are boundless; I vow to enter them. The buddha way is unsurpassable; I vow to realize it

LATE NIGHT

SAFETY WATCHPatrol the household to ensure all locks are secured, windows are closed, and lights and appliances are off.

SLEEP PRACTICERecite with hands in gassho: “Going to sleep, the Bodhisattva inspires all beings to have bodies that attain peace and hearts without disturbance.” Sleep on the right side in lion’s posture. Count breaths to 100, then backwards, until asleep.
